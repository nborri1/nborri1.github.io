How to read the estimates

The units. Each value is the percentage-point difference in weekly log excess return associated with a one-standard-deviation higher AI factor, holding the market return constant. A positive number means positive historical comovement after the market control; a negative number means the reverse. It does not measure how much AI the firm uses or predict its return.

The method. The AI factor combines standardized weekly log growth in tokens, spending and active users on OpenRouter. Rolling regressions use 13-week windows and require at least nine observations. We retain the research pipeline's treatment of extreme observations and full-sample factor construction. All values use the same factor scaling. Average exposure is an equally weighted average of each stock's valid rolling estimates. The chart's vertical scale adjusts to the selected stocks, and both lines share it. Missing estimates are left as gaps.

Industry filters use the broad Fama–French ten-industry classification.

Interpretation. These are noisy point estimates, with no confidence intervals shown. A difference between two values is not a test of statistical significance. Co-movement does not establish that AI caused a stock's return. OpenRouter's user base is nonrandom and relatively sophisticated. The data here are company-level sensitivities, not underlying stock-return histories or OpenRouter account records.

Source: Borri, Liu and Tsyvinski, updated research using the methodology of our working paper, with licensed OpenRouter data and CRSP. The public paper may use an earlier sample.
