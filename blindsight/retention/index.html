Share of first-time adopters retained

At week eleven, retention is the share of users active on that same model in that week. Our working paper provides additional details on the methodology.

How to read the earlier weeks

At an earlier week, the curve counts users active on the same model in that week or later through week eleven. For example, week one measures any return in weeks 1–11. The adoption week is week zero.

What the comparison can tell us

Each account receives equal weight. The curves measure continued consumption of a specific model through OpenRouter; they do not measure revenue retention or activity through other channels. Different launch dates, adopter mixes and replacement models affect the comparison: same-model retention falls when a lab releases a successor inside the window, so labs that ship quickly look worse here than in the post’s provider figure.

The selection takes, for each major lab, the most recently released model with at least 20,000 first-time accounts that can be followed for eleven weeks, adds earlier flagships of Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and DeepSeek and the six models of our working paper’s figure, and includes Claude Fable 5 at the authors’ request although its launch-week cohort is smaller. Where two models were released in the same week, both are included. Qwen’s most recent releases are API-only, so its largest open-weight model of the most recent open-weight family stands in. Open-weight and closed-source labels follow the paper’s classification. These are historical observations, not a ranking of today’s models. We will report the retention of the very latest releases, such as Claude Fable 5 and Astra, in full as soon as a long enough window of data is available.
