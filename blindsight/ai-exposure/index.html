Definitions, sources and the case for a futures market

Each regression explains the selected series using an intercept and the factors you choose. R² is historical in-sample explanatory power. Adjusted R² accounts for the number of selected factors and can be negative. Confidence intervals and intercept p-values use Newey–West standard errors with five lags, a finite-sample correction and Student-t inference. Clearing all factors leaves an intercept-only model: its intercept is the sample mean and explained variation is zero. Comparing many factor combinations is exploratory; the displayed significance levels are not adjusted for multiple testing. Separate significance tests do not establish that intercepts differ across specifications.

The standard six are the US market, size, value, profitability, investment and momentum factors. NVIDIA is its weekly stock return in excess of the risk-free rate. You can select any combination of these seven controls. The baseline cards always use the standard six; GPU cards additionally show all seven. Under the standard six, the AI portfolio's historical alpha is 0.51% per week (p = 0.014), before trading and shorting costs.

The AI factor is the existing research PCA score combining standardized weekly log growth in tokens, spending and active users from licensed OpenRouter data. OpenRouter users are a nonrandom, relatively sophisticated sample. The factor uses full-sample weights and centring; its intercept depends on that normalization. A futures settlement benchmark would need rules fixed or governed prospectively, with published data and revision policies.

A zero-mean AI factor does not automatically imply a zero regression intercept: intercept = mean AI − fitted exposures × mean equity factors. If both the outcome and all controls are demeaned over the regression sample, the intercept becomes zero mechanically, while slopes, residuals and R² are unchanged. Our factor is centred over its full research construction sample, not this shorter regression sample. Its displayed intercept is descriptive; it does not measure an investment premium.

The three growth components are measured as 100 × the change in the natural logarithm. A log percentage point is approximately a percentage point for small changes. Positive growth intercepts are not investment alpha, nor evidence by themselves of a risk premium. The long–short portfolio buys the highest AI-beta quintile and shorts the lowest, with value weights and prior-week estimated exposures. It is an updated research portfolio through June 2026, before trading and shorting costs, not a live investment record.

The paper calls the associated historical return spread the AI Premium. These tests indicate exposure beyond the benchmark portfolios examined; they do not establish risk compensation rather than cash-flow news or guarantee a useful hedge. A consumption-linked contract could address AI diffusion, while GPU futures address computing rental costs. Neither contract's usefulness depends on its benchmark showing positive investment alpha.

Equity indices and consumption comparisons have 129 weeks from January 8, 2024. The portfolio has 120 weeks from March 11, 2024. Both GPU indices use the same 47 weeks, August 4, 2025–June 26, 2026, with identical dates for every factor combination. All comparisons end in the last complete week of the underlying stock-return research data: June 26 for stocks and rental prices; June 28 for Monday–Sunday consumption.

Morgan Stanley AI Power Generation (MSXXAIPW) targets companies benefiting from growing power consumption and data-centre usage. It is distinct from Solactive Global AI (SOLGLAI), offered through Morgan Stanley products. SOLGLAI is an EUR price index: we convert its levels to USD using the Federal Reserve's daily USD-per-EUR exchange rate, carrying forward the latest available fixing on holidays. We retain the index's built-in 1.2% annual management decrement. The equity comparisons use index-level changes without adding dividends and subtract the USD risk-free rate once.

Futures would make exposure to the settlement index tradable. Their profits and losses would come from changes in quoted futures prices; the index's historical intercept cannot identify the alpha of a contract that did not yet trade. A useful hedging contract does not require a positive average payoff.

GPU series are rental-price benchmarks, not holding returns from CME futures. They belong to the Silicon Data benchmark families in CME's announcement; the supplied B200 ticker is not verified identical to the announced contract's settlement series. Some index history predates public launch announcements and may be backtested. Different windows and the portfolio's long–short construction limit cross-series comparisons.
